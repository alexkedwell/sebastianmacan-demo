Gloss: the four knob mixing plugin that makes tracks sit right

Gloss is mixing for producers who never wanted an engineering degree. Tell it what it is hearing, vocal, drums, bass or master, and Gloss tunes everything under the hood for that job. Then you shape the sound with four knobs: CLEAN clears mud and boxiness, PUNCH brings out the hit and snap, GLOW adds the expensive top end, and WIDTH opens the stereo image.

Under the hood it combines EQ shaping, compression and saturation into those four musical controls, so instead of tweaking ten plugins you turn a couple of knobs until the track sits right. The whole time, auto-gain keeps the volume honest, so louder never fools your ears into thinking something improved when it just got loud.

How producers use Gloss

  • On vocals: a little CLEAN to stop the fight with the instrumental, PUNCH to bring the voice forward, GLOW for air. Three moves and the vocal sits.
  • On a drum bus: PUNCH wakes up lazy samples, CLEAN removes the cardboard box, and the loop suddenly knocks.
  • Because auto-gain matches the volume, whatever sounds better with Gloss actually is better. Trust the first instinct and move on.

Frequently asked questions

What does Gloss actually do to my mix?

It combines EQ, compression and saturation behind four knobs, with per-track-type tuning. You describe the track, turn CLEAN, PUNCH, GLOW and WIDTH, and stop when it sounds good.

Is Gloss good for beginners?

It was built for exactly that. There are no frequency charts, ratios or attack times to learn; the four knobs are the whole manual.

Can I use Gloss on the master bus?

Yes. Pick the master mode and use gentle amounts of GLOW and WIDTH for final polish before mastering.

How is Gloss different from a channel strip?

A channel strip hands you the raw tools: EQ bands, thresholds, ratios. Gloss hands you results. The per-track-type tuning means CLEAN on a vocal does a different job than CLEAN on a bass, so every knob move is already musical.
