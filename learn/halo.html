Halo: mastering with one knob and a golden ring

Halo is a mastering finisher that reduces the whole mastering rack to one decision. Pick a target, Streaming, Club or Podcast, each tuned to the loudness and tone that world expects, then turn the LIFT knob until the golden halo ring closes around the target. A closed ring means you have arrived: loud, glued and release-ready, with a true-peak limiter on the door so nothing clips and nothing gets penalized by streaming platforms.

Behind the single knob, Halo chains the essentials of a mastering rack: gain staging, glue compression that makes the track feel like one finished thing, a sheen lift tuned for masters, final stereo width polish, and true-peak limiting. It goes at the very end of your master chain, after everything else.

How producers use Halo

  • Finish a track, put Halo last on the master, pick Streaming, turn LIFT until the ring closes, bounce, upload. That is the entire workflow.
  • Master to the Streaming target, then flip to Club and bounce a second, harder version for DJ sets. Two masters in thirty seconds.
  • Podcasters and video editors use the Podcast target to land spoken word at consistent, platform-safe loudness without reading a single LUFS spec.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really master a track with one knob?

For streaming-ready loudness and polish, yes. Halo hides the gain staging, glue, sheen, width and limiting behind LIFT, and the closing halo shows you the sweet spot visually.

What is a true-peak limiter and why does it matter?

It stops the loudest peaks from clipping even after digital-to-analog conversion, which is what streaming services check. Halo handles it automatically so your upload never gets flagged or distorted.

Do I still need mastering knowledge to use Halo?

No. The targets encode the loudness standards for you. If you know mastering, treat Halo as the fastest possible reference finisher.
