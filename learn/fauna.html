Fauna: the synth that lets you play whales, birds and stranger things

Fauna turns animal voices into a real playable instrument. Six species live inside it, from blue whale moans to songbird trills and loon calls, and you play them from a keyboard like any synth: low notes moan, high notes cry, held notes let the creature breathe, and chords sound like a flock. Instead of classic synth waves, Fauna models living, organic voices.

GLIDE slides between notes the way real animals do, and TRILL adds the flutter that makes birdsong sound alive. BREATH and BODY shape how airy or full the voice is, ATTACK and RELEASE take you from ambient swells to fast expressive calls, and the OCEAN knob puts the whole thing underwater in one move: distant, deep and instantly cinematic.

How producers use Fauna

  • Layer a whale two octaves under a pad, drench it in OCEAN, and you have a sub layer with a soul. It reads as sound design, not as a preset.
  • Play songbird trills over a lo-fi or ambient beat for organic top-end movement that no sample pack loop will match, because it follows your notes.
  • Film and game composers use Fauna for instant creature beds: a held low cluster with slow attack is an underwater scene in one track.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of synth is Fauna?

A sound design instrument that models organic, living voices, bird chirps, whale calls and more, rather than sawtooth and square waves. It suits ambient music, film scoring, game audio and ear candy in any genre.

Is Fauna a sample pack?

No. It is a playable instrument. The voices respond to your notes, glide between pitches and sustain as long as you hold the key, which loops and one-shots cannot do.

What formats does Fauna come in?

VST3 and AU instrument for macOS, loading in Ableton Live, Logic Pro, FL Studio, GarageBand, Reaper, Studio One and more. Windows is coming soon.

How much does Fauna cost?

Fauna is 29 dollars, and it is free to download during the launch window.
