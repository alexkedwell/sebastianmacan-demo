Reels: the tape machine plugin from pristine studio to dusty cassette

Reels is a tape machine in the box, with the reels actually spinning on screen. It models the things that made tape sound like music: asymmetric saturation that thickens the harder you push it, the head bump low-end lift that gives drums vintage weight, and the gentle wow and flutter pitch drift that makes digital audio feel human.

Three machines cover three decades: a clean 15ips studio deck, a warm workhorse, and a genuinely old cassette found in a drawer. AGE packs years of wear into one knob, dulling the top and softening the edges, while HISS doses real tape noise to taste for lo-fi beds and intros. Push the input until you smile; the harder you hit the tape, the thicker it gets.

How producers use Reels

  • Start on the master bus with the studio machine for instant glue that makes a digital mix feel finished.
  • Print the drum bus through the cassette machine with medium AGE, then blend it under the clean bus for lo-fi weight without losing punch.
  • Automate WOW and FLUTTER up during intros and outros so the song literally warps in and out like a found memory.

Frequently asked questions

What does tape emulation add to a track?

Warm saturation, softened digital edges, a low-end head bump and slight pitch drift. Together they are a huge part of the lo-fi and vintage sound, and gentle settings simply make mixes feel glued.

Is Reels good for lo-fi hip hop?

The cassette machine with AGE and HISS turned up is practically a lo-fi preset. It covers everything from subtle vintage color to a warped 30-year-old tape.

What formats does Reels come in?

VST3 and AU for macOS: Ableton Live, Logic Pro, FL Studio, GarageBand, Reaper, Studio One and more. Windows builds are coming soon.

What is the difference between wow and flutter?

Both are pitch drift from the physical tape transport. Wow is the slow, seasick drift over seconds; flutter is the fast shimmer on top. A little of each makes things feel human, a lot sounds like a warped memory.
