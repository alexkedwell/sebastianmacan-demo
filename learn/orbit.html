What is an auto-pan plugin?

An auto-pan plugin moves a sound between the left and right speakers automatically, in time with the music. It is a panning effect driven by an LFO, used for stereo movement on hi-hats, arps, pads, guitars and vocals.

A 3D auto-pan adds front-to-back distance as well. As the sound goes behind you it gets darker and slightly later in one ear, the same cues your ears use in a real room. That is what makes a sound feel like it circles your head on headphones.

How to make the 8D audio effect

8D audio is the name given to tracks where the music circles the listener's head on headphones. It is the same family of effect: circular panning with depth. Orbit gives you that movement as a plugin, tempo-synced and automatable in your DAW. Put it on a track or on the whole mix and it works as an 8D audio maker inside your own project.

How to use Orbit in three steps

  1. Put Orbit on a track. Hi-hats, arps, textures, ad-libs: anything you want flying in circles.
  2. Press play. The orbit visual shows exactly where your sound is right now.
  3. Set the RATE. It is tempo-synced by default so the orbit locks to your song, or switch to Free and set any speed in Hz.

Every control

  • RATE. How fast the sound circles.
  • SYNC. Lock the orbit to bars and beats, or free-run for drifting ambient motion.
  • WIDTH. How wide the circle is. Narrow is subtle movement, wide is a full flyby.
  • DEPTH. Front-to-back distance. This is what makes it 3D instead of ping-pong.
  • TILT. Leans the path, like a ring around a planet.

Ideas to try

  • Put Orbit on your hat bus at a slow 4-bar rate with medium width. The groove starts breathing.
  • Automate WIDTH from zero to full across a breakdown so an arp lifts off the centre and starts circling as the section opens.
  • Use fast, narrow orbits on ad-libs and FX for spinning ear candy on headphones.
  • Free-run a slow orbit on an ambient pad for stereo movement that never repeats on the bar.
