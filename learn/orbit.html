Orbit: the 3D auto-pan plugin that flies sound around your head

Orbit moves sound around the listener in real 3D, not just left to right. Instead of a simple pan LFO, Orbit combines darkening, micro-delay and doppler cues so the sound seems to physically travel behind and in front of your head, the way things do in real space. It is headphone candy first, but the movement still translates on speakers.

The live orbit visual shows exactly where your sound is at every moment, so what you see is what you hear. RATE is tempo-synced by default, locking the circle to your song, or you can free-run it in Hz for drifting ambient motion. WIDTH sets how wide the circle is, DEPTH adds the front-to-back distance that makes it 3D instead of ping-pong, and TILT leans the whole path like a ring around a planet.

How producers use Orbit

  • Put Orbit on a hat bus at a slow 4-bar rate with medium width. The groove starts breathing and listeners feel the motion without ever spotting it.
  • Automate WIDTH from zero to full across a breakdown so an arp slowly lifts off the center and starts circling right as the section opens up.
  • Use fast, narrow orbits on ad-libs and FX for the spinning 8D-style ear candy that does numbers on headphones and short-form video.

Frequently asked questions

What does an auto-pan plugin do?

It moves sound between the left and right speakers automatically, in time with the music. Orbit goes further by adding depth and height cues, so the sound feels like it circles the listener instead of bouncing between two speakers.

Is this the same as 8D audio?

It is the same family of effect. 8D audio edits are usually made by manually panning a finished song; Orbit gives you that circling movement as a plugin, tempo-synced and automatable inside your DAW.

What formats does Orbit come in?

VST3 and AU for macOS: Ableton Live, Logic Pro, FL Studio, GarageBand, Reaper, Studio One and more. Windows builds are coming soon.
