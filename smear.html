What Smear does

Smear takes whatever you feed it and melts it. The incoming sound gets cut into a cloud of grains, some of them thrown up an octave or a fifth, smudged by a feedback delay, then poured into a plate reverb that runs from a short room to a 14 second wash. A dry drum loop turns into a pad. A vocal turns into a choir in the next room. Turn SMEAR up and the edges go, turn WASH up and the whole thing dissolves, hit FREEZE and the tail hangs there for as long as you want it.

Melty, the readout

The violet creature in the panel is the meter. It drips with the wash and melts harder the more you smear, so you can see how far gone the sound is before you hear it.

Ten controls, one cloud

SMEAR. How dense the grain cloud is. Zero is clean, full is soup.
PITCH. Chance a grain plays +12, +7 or -12 semitones. A little is sparkle, a lot is a choir.
BLUR. A feedback delay that smudges the grains. Low keeps the groove, high turns rhythm into texture.
WASH. Reverb amount behind the cloud.
DECAY. Reverb tail, 0.6 s to 14 s.
DEPTH. Predelay, early/late balance and a high-cut in one move, so the verb sits further back instead of on top.
SHIMMER. A +12 st pitch shift in the reverb feedback. Every pass climbs an octave.
TONE. Dark to bright on the wet sound only.
DRIFT. Slow wobble of grain pitch and delay time, so a long tail never sits still.
FREEZE. Holds the grains and an endless reverb while lit. Play over it, then let go.

Get started in 3 steps

1. Put Smear on something. A drum loop, a chord stab, a vocal phrase. It opens on Init, already wet.
2. Turn WASH and DECAY up. The sound starts to pour out behind itself. Stop where it still feels like your track.
3. Hit FREEZE at the right moment. The tail hangs. Automate it on a transition and let it go on the drop.

Make it yours

MIX and OUT. Blend the cloud with the dry sound, trim the result. BYPASS pill in the header for an honest A/B.
ON pill. The switch in the panel corner. Off dims the box and passes the dry signal, same as inside the mangler.
INIT ANY KNOB. Right-click or Cmd-click a knob to send it home.
Window size. Opens compact. Drag the corner grip to make it as big as you like; your DAW remembers it.
