Drums that sound like they were found, not made

Clean drums are the thing that gives a lofi beat away. The kick is too tight, the snare is too bright, the hats sparkle like they came out of a sample pack this morning. Honey is the tape machine in between. Put it on the drum bus and the transients soften, the top end wears down, the hits glue into one old recording with a little hiss and a little wobble. Same groove, same swing, just thirty years older. Push DRIVE and WARMTH and it gets thick and crunchy. Keep them low and it is just the dust.

What Honey does

Honey is what happens to your drums when they get printed to old tape. It is built on real tape physics: the record and playback emphasis curves, magnetic hysteresis, tape compression, head bump, high-frequency loss, wow and flutter and hiss, all modelled instead of faked. Hits round off, the kit glues together and the whole loop sits back like it came off a cassette somebody found in a drawer. It works on chords and pads too, at your own discretion. Drums are where it shines.

Get started in 3 steps

1. Put Honey on your drum bus. The whole kit, or just the snare. It already sounds older with nothing touched.
2. Pick a machine. STUDIO is a two-inch deck at 15 ips: expensive glue. CASSETTE is the default: moderate hiss, a little wobble. WALKMAN is a worn tape on dying batteries: heavy wow, dropouts, a ceiling around 9 kHz. The whole face changes with it.
3. Turn WARMTH up, then DRIVE until it crunches. WOBBLE for movement, AGE for years of wear, HISS for the room tone. Save it as a preset when it is yours.

Make it yours

DRIVE. How hard you hit the tape. Below 12 o'clock is glue. Past 12 it climbs hard: soft saturation that stays smooth because the highs saturate first, all the way to crushed tape at the top, level-matched so it never just gets louder.
WARMTH. Head bump and low-mid weight, tuned per machine. Where the kick gets its weight and the snare gets its body. Past 12 o'clock the top end rolls off and the lows swell until it sounds like the tape is melting.
WOBBLE. Wow and flutter. A little is human, a lot is a memory. Past 12 o'clock it goes seasick: nearly four semitones of drift at the top.
HISS. Real tape noise, dosed to taste, machine-dependent. Cassette is louder, Studio is faint.
AGE. Years of wear. Dulls the top, adds dropouts, softens everything. Crank it and the tape starts falling apart: dropouts every few hundred milliseconds and a top end that is barely there.
MIX and OUTPUT. Parallel blend and level. Same positions on every face, so your hands always know where they are. Leave MIX halfway on a chord loop: the tape path drifts against the dry signal and the chords start to phase. It is a glitch we kept on purpose. Automate MIX slowly and it becomes a flanger that breathes with the tape.
PRESETS + INIT. Preset pill in the header, 16 factory sounds (Worn, Walkman '94, Chewed and Crushed live at the loud end), save unlimited of your own. Right-click, two-finger tap or Cmd-click any knob for INIT: that one control snaps back to the preset you loaded.
READOUT. Grab any knob and a small readout appears in the bottom-right corner showing exactly where you are (66%, 67%, -3.5 dB). Let go and it fades. Every knob's bottom half is the original Honey sound, so your old sessions and presets sound exactly the same.

Pro tip

On lofi drums. CASSETTE. Turn WARMTH all the way up, then bring DRIVE up while dropping OUTPUT a little. Add a touch of AGE. Nice and tight, old and warm, ready to loop.

On the snare alone. WALKMAN, DRIVE past 12, WOBBLE low. The crack wears down into a thud that sits in the beat instead of on top of it.

On chords. STUDIO, DRIVE around 11 o'clock, WOBBLE just past off. Smooth glue, no dirt. Use at your own discretion, it is the drums that Honey was made for.
