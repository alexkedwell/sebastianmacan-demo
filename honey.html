What Honey does

Honey is what happens to a sound when it gets printed to old tape. It is built on real tape physics: the record and playback emphasis curves, magnetic hysteresis, tape compression, head bump, high-frequency loss, wow and flutter and hiss, all modelled instead of faked. The noise is real too: actual cassette hiss, or the crackle beds pulled off two samplers. Transients round off, harsh top end wears down, the low end gets weight and everything glues together like it came off a cassette somebody found in a drawer. Drums, samples, keys, guitars, vocals and full mixes are all fair game.

What makes the Honey sound

One machine, modelled end to end. Record curve, tape, playback curve. The saturation, the head bump, the wear and the wobble all come from the same place, which is why Honey holds together at any setting. Nudge one knob and the whole machine responds like a machine would.
The noise is real. HISS is actual cassette noise, not a synthetic bed. SM-404 and SM-303 are crackle beds pulled off two samplers. Pick the one that matches what you grew up on, and sit it under anything.
The knobs move like hardware. Grab WOBBLE or WOW on a real deck and the pitch glides to the new speed, it does not jump. Honey does the same, so a fast knob move is always musical, never a lurch or a click. Automate it and it stays smooth.
PHASE is a playable knob. The wet tape path drifts against the dry signal, and Honey puts that drift under your hand: a slow breathing flange that follows the tape, from a hint of movement to a full chorus wash. Sweep it while chords play.
Character and smoothness in the same box. Grit, dropouts and crackle when you want them, warm tape compression and a soft top end when you do not. Dial anywhere between. 16 presets cover both ends, $15, yours forever, resizable, no subscription.

Make it yours

DRIVE. How hard you hit the tape. Below 12 o'clock is glue. Past 12 it climbs hard: soft saturation that stays smooth because the highs saturate first, all the way to crushed tape at the top, level-matched so it never just gets louder.
WARMTH. Head bump and low-mid weight, tuned per machine. Where the kick gets its weight and the snare gets its body. Past 12 o'clock the top end rolls off and the lows swell until it sounds like the tape is melting.
WOBBLE, WOW and FLUTTER. WOBBLE is the overall wow and flutter: a little is human, a lot is a memory, and past 12 o'clock it goes seasick. WOW and WOW RATE set the slow pitch drift, FLUTTER and FLUT RATE the fast shiver, RATE drives the whole motor. The movement knobs glide the way a real tape machine would, so a fast grab never lurches.
HISS. Real tape noise or sampler crackle, dosed to taste. Pick the bed in the pill: TAPE, SM-404 or SM-303.
AGE. Years of wear. Dulls the top, adds dropouts, softens everything. Crank it and the tape starts falling apart: dropouts every few hundred milliseconds and a top end that is barely there.
PHASE, MIX and OUTPUT. MIX is a clean parallel blend before the tape, OUTPUT is the level. PHASE is the trick: with WOBBLE, WOW or AGE turned up, leave PHASE halfway on a chord loop and the tape path drifts against the dry signal until the chords start to phase. It is a glitch we kept on purpose. Automate PHASE slowly and it becomes a flanger that breathes with the tape.
PRESETS + INIT. Preset pill in the header, 16 factory sounds (Worn, Walkman '94, Chewed and Crushed live at the loud end), save unlimited of your own. Right-click, two-finger tap or Cmd-click any knob for INIT: that one control snaps back to the preset you loaded.
READOUT and RESIZE. Grab any knob and a small readout appears in the bottom-left corner showing exactly where you are (66%, 67%, -3.5 dB). Let go and it fades. Drag the bottom-right corner to make the whole plugin bigger or smaller. Your old sessions and presets load exactly as they were saved.

Pro tip

On samples. WARMTH up, DRIVE to 11 o'clock, AGE a touch, SM-404 hiss. The chop sounds like it came off a record you own, not a pack you downloaded.

On Rhodes, keys and chords. DRIVE around 10 o'clock, WOBBLE just past off, WARMTH to taste. Smooth glue, slow movement, no dirt. Leave PHASE halfway with WOBBLE up and the chords start to breathe.

On drums. WARMTH all the way up, then DRIVE up while dropping OUTPUT a little. Tight, old and warm. For the snare alone, DRIVE past 12 and the crack wears down into a thud that sits in the beat.

On the mix bus. Everything low: DRIVE under 10 o'clock, WOBBLE off, HISS off, a touch of WARMTH. Nothing obvious happens, the whole track just sits down and smooths out. Bypass it and you will miss it.
