What Bloom does

Bloom is the melodic house glow machine. Drop it on a dry chord, a plucky synth, a piano loop, and turn one knob. Shimmer opens above the sound, reverb wraps around it, slow motion starts drifting through it. Then BREATHE makes the whole thing pump in time with your track, that sidechain sunrise feel that carries an Anjunadeep set at 6am. No routing, no ghost kick track, no five-plugin chain.

The knobs

BLOOM. The hero. One macro that opens shimmer, reverb and motion together. At zero the sound is dry. At full it is a sunrise.
BREATHE. Tempo-synced sidechain-style pumping, no kick input needed. It reads your host BPM and ducks the wash in time, so pads breathe with the groove.
RATE. How long each breath is: 1/1, 1/2, 1/4 or 1/8. Whole-bar swells for intros, 1/4 for that classic club pump.
WARMTH. Tape saturation on the tail. Turns glassy digital shimmer into something golden and a little worn.
WIDTH. Mono-safe stereo widener. Big on headphones, still solid when the PA folds to mono.
MOTION. A slow evolving LFO drift through the texture, so ten seconds of held pad never sounds like a freeze frame.
MIX. Dry/wet. Parallel glow at 30 percent, full ambient wash at 100.
BYPASS. Instant A/B so you can hear what the sunrise is doing.

Eight sunrises in the box

Factory presets tuned like set moments, not init patches: Anjuna Sunrise, Boehmer Breath, Cavern Bloom, Warm Tape Pad, Festival Pump, Slow Aurora, Gentle Halo and Deep Field. Load one, play a chord, adjust BLOOM to taste.

What to put it on

Plain triad pads become the main event. Pianos turn into 6am festival pianos. Plucks get a glowing tail that pumps with the beat. Vocals get a halo. Even a single sustained note starts to feel like a track.
