What Mulch does

Drag up to 8 samples into the chipper. Any samples. A drum break, a vocal chop, a piano note, a recording of birds outside your window. Mulch chews the whole pile into grains and births ONE new playable instrument out of everything you fed it. Every key you press regrows the sound from random little pieces of your pile, so no two notes are ever exactly the same.

The knobs

MULCH. The big one. At zero, notes grow from your first sample only. Turn it up and the grains start coming from EVERYTHING in the pile at once. Full mulch = total hybrid.
CHEW. Grain size. Tiny confetti pieces for shimmering clouds, big chunks for recognizable slices of your source.
SPIN. Chaos. Random pitch throws up to a fifth in either direction, plus a chance any grain plays backwards. A little is texture, a lot is a haunted blender.
GLUE. Melts the grains into one voice: a warming lowpass plus a soft squash so the pile stops sounding like pieces and starts sounding like an instrument.
ATTACK / RELEASE. Simple amp envelope. Slow attack = evolving pads, fast = playable keys.
OUT. Output trim.

Watch it eat

The chipper on the front panel is not decoration. Your samples sit in the tray as colored chips, and when you play, the gears grind and the chute sprays grain confetti in each chip's color, so you can literally see whose pieces are in the sound right now. Click a chip to take it out of the pile.

What to feed it

Melodic stuff at similar pitches mulches into playable hybrids. Drums plus anything = instant texture percussion. One vocal plus one synth is the classic. Field recordings turn everything into a film score. There is no wrong pile.
